3.0 Turn Sequence

Each turn represents one day of operations and unfolds through eleven phases, always in the same order. The rules in the preceding and following chapters constantly say "in Phase X" or "during Phase Y": this chapter is the reference point that defines which phases exist, in what order they follow one another, and exactly what happens in each.

Respecting the order of phases is not a bureaucratic detail: it is what makes the system predictable and verifiable by both players. An action declared in the wrong phase, or paid for after the correct phase has already passed, is not valid — consistent with the Golden Rule of chapter 1.0. In disputed cases, the question "which phase are we in?" almost always settles the doubt.

3.1 General Turn Structure

# Phase What happens, in brief
1Weather and WindThe turn's weather conditions are determined.
2LogisticsLogistics Points are received, stored, and spent.
3OrdersOrders are written in secret, revealed together, and Operational Initiative is determined.
4AviationAir missions are resolved: fighters, reconnaissance, observation, interdiction.
5Artillery and GasBombardments, counter-battery fire, harassing fire, and gas missions are resolved.
6MovementDivisions move: internal movement, operational movement, reserve entry.
7CombatDeclared assaults are resolved: defensive fire, attack, trench combat.
8Retreats and advancesForced retreats, advances after combat, routs.
9Isolation and supplyIt is checked which divisions are supplied and which are isolated; any surrender tests.
10RecoveryEligible divisions remove Fatigue.
11End of turnVictory check and recording of the state for the next turn.

Some actions, as seen in chapter 2.0, span more than one phase: Reserve Entry is paid for in Logistics, ordered in Orders, and completed in Movement; gas is checked in the Weather and Wind Phase, paid for in Logistics, ordered in Orders, and resolved in Artillery and Gas. This is not an inconsistency: it simply means the action has a "commitment moment" (when the player commits to doing it) that is different from its "resolution moment" (when the effect is applied on the map).

3.2 Weather and Wind Phase

The first thing established is the turn's conditions. Weather mainly affects aviation (see chapter 11.0), while Wind mainly affects gas missions (see chapter 10.0). Unless the scenario in play states otherwise, both are determined by rolling 1d12 on the tables below, before the players know how many LP they will receive or what they will write in their orders: this way no one can decide an attack plan already knowing whether the sky will be clear or whether the wind will favor gas.

1d12 (Weather)Result
1-2Fog or mist
3-6Rain or mud
7-12Clear
1d12 (Wind)Result
1-6Favorable
7-9Variable
10-11Strong
12Adverse
Note: Wind only matters in turns where at least one player intends to declare a Gas mission; it can still be noted every turn for convenience, so it does not have to be rolled at the last moment if a gas plan is decided as a surprise. A scenario may replace these tables with fixed conditions or with its own seasonal table, as indicated in its special rules.

3.3 Logistics Phase

Each player applies the procedure of chapter 6.1: receives the turn's LP, adds them to those stored, checks the maximum stockpile, and pays for all the actions he intends to carry out during the turn that have a LP cost — bombardments, gas, air missions, operational movement, reserve entry, replacements, fortifications, extra recovery. This is where, in practice, the most important decision of the turn is made: how to distribute a limited resource across every desired operation. Unspent LP remain available next turn, up to the maximum allowed by the scenario.

3.4 Orders Phase

Both players secretly write all of the turn's orders, following the guidelines of chapter 7.1 (movement, attack, voluntary retreat, artillery, gas, aviation, reserve). A 0 LP action, such as a base attack, still needs to be written here: no division carries out an unordered action on its own initiative. Once writing is finished, the orders are revealed simultaneously. Immediately after the reveal, the turn's Operational Initiative is determined, following the procedure of chapter 7.2: both players roll 1d12, apply modifiers, and the higher result wins. Initiative does not change orders already written: it only decides the resolution order when two events of the turn are in conflict, as illustrated in chapters 7.3, 7.4, and 8.3.

3.5 Aviation Phase

All air missions are resolved, following the procedure of chapter 11.2: first fighter duels and fighter modifiers (escort, interception, local superiority), then reconnaissance, artillery observation, and interdiction. The internal order is not arbitrary: a successful escort or interception must be known before resolving the mission it protects or disrupts, which is why it is resolved first. Artillery observation that succeeds in this phase remains "pending" and is applied as a modifier when the linked artillery mission is resolved in the following phase.

3.6 Artillery and Gas Phase

Declared artillery missions are resolved in sequence — preparatory bombardment, rolling barrage, counter-battery fire, harassing fire — along with gas missions, applying the observation bonuses obtained in the previous phase and the effects of the wind determined at the start of the turn (chapters 9.0 and 10.0). Immediate effects, such as the suppression of machine guns or the reduction of a fortification, apply at once and remain valid for the rest of the turn; bonuses intended for an assault (such as the extra success from a rolling barrage) remain assigned and will be used in the Combat Phase.

3.7 Movement Phase

Divisions with orders for internal or operational movement move, following chapter 8.0; reserves that have been paid for and ordered enter the map in Line 3 of the entry sector, following chapter 6.3. Any successful air interdiction or harassing fire from the previous phase may already have made this movement more costly or more risky. Divisions that have moved in this phase remain subject to the restrictions of chapter 8.2: in particular, a division that entered from reserve, or that carried out operational movement while at Fatigue 3 or higher, still cannot take part in an assault the same turn.

3.8 Combat Phase

All assaults declared in the Orders Phase are resolved, one at a time or in an order agreed by the players when there are no direct dependencies between them. For each assault, the procedure of chapter 12.0 applies: first the defensive fire of unsuppressed machine guns, then the comparison of successes between attacker and defender, then, if the result allows it, trench combat. It is in this phase that the effects prepared in the previous phases become concrete: a successful bombardment, a gas mission that weakened the line, or a rolling barrage that adds a success all make the difference right here.

3.9 Retreats and Advances Phase

Divisions that must retreat forcibly due to a combat result, per chapter 13.2, do so now, following the retreat routes of chapter 13.3; victorious divisions entitled to do so advance into the conquered line, as described in chapter 12.7 and the movement actions table. Voluntary retreats ordered in the Orders Phase, if not already resolved due to Operational Initiative (chapter 7.4), are completed here.

3.10 Isolation and Supply Phase

With the turn's positions now final, it is checked for every division on the map whether there is a path of friendly sectors through to a valid rear area, per chapter 14.4. Divisions found isolated suffer the prescribed effects (no replacements, no artillery support, -1 Morale and +1 Fatigue) and, if already at Morale 1 or Fatigue 5, take the surrender test of chapter 13.7 in this phase.

3.11 Recovery Phase

Divisions that meet the conditions of chapter 14.5 — not in Line 1, not contested, not isolated, not bombarded, not committed to an attack this turn — remove 1 Fatigue for free. By spending 1 additional LP, already allocated during the Logistics Phase, a division may remove 1 more point of Fatigue. This is the only phase in which the state of units improves rather than worsens: it represents rest, the rotation of guard duty, and the reorganizing of units away from direct contact.

3.12 End of Turn

The victory check called for by the current scenario is carried out (chapter 16.0 for Verdun, chapter 18.0 for Isonzo, chapter 20.0 for Messines), cumulative losses, surrendered or routed divisions, and any other data required by the victory conditions are recorded. The next turn begins again from the Weather and Wind Phase.

Summary example: it is Turn 1 of the Verdun scenario. Weather (Clear) and Wind (Favorable) are rolled. In Logistics, the German receives the turn's LP plus the 6 special preparation LP and devotes them to bombardment, observation, and escort. In Orders, both players write their plans and reveal them; the German automatically has Initiative in Turn 1 by a special scenario rule. In Aviation, the escort is resolved and then the observation. In Artillery, the German bombardment, aided by the observation, hits on 9+ instead of 10+ and suppresses some French machine guns. In Movement, the German divisions move into assault position. In Combat, the attack on C2 Beaumont L1 is resolved. In Retreats and Advances, the Frenchman, if defeated, falls back, or the German advances. In Isolation and Supply, the divisions involved are checked. In Recovery, uncommitted units remove Fatigue. In End of Turn, any pace-related VP called for by the scenario are calculated.
