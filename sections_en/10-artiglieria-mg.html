9.0 Machine Guns and Artillery

Machine guns and artillery are the tactical core of the Western Front. MGs punish movement in the open; artillery prepares, isolates, suppresses, and wears down.

These two weapons have complementary, almost opposite roles in the game system. The machine gun is a defensive, economical weapon: it costs no LP to use, hits with relatively high precision (9+), but only if it has not already been suppressed, which is why suppressing it is almost always the first goal of any attack preparation. Artillery, by contrast, is an offensive weapon that costs LP, is less precise per single roll (10+, or 9+ with observation), but can roll many dice in a single mission and produce effects that stack — suppression, reduction of fortifications, Fatigue, morale tests. Chapter 9.0 covers both together because, in practice, their use is always intertwined: a bombardment is fired to suppress enemy machine guns, and one counts one's own surviving machine guns to know how much defensive fire can be opposed to the assault that will follow.

Artillery at Verdun: the German offensive of 21 February 1916 was preceded by an exceptionally intense bombardment. In the game, this central role is represented by LP, aerial observation, counter-battery fire, and artillery's ability to suppress MGs and reduce fortifications.

9.1 Machine Guns

MG action Cost Feasible if... Procedure Effect
Fire in the sector 0 LP MG not suppressed, line not contested, valid target. Every MG rolls 2d12; success on 9+. Adds defensive successes.
Adjacent support 0 LP MG not suppressed, connected sector, line not contested. Every MG rolls 1d12; success on 9+. Adds reduced defensive successes.
Moving an MG 1 LP Starting and arrival lines controlled, not contested. Pay 1 LP and write the order. The MG moves but does not fire that turn.

9.2 Artillery: Base Rule

All artillery missions — whether preparatory, barrage, counter-battery, or simple harassment — share the same basic mechanic described here: a number of dice proportional to the assigned batteries is rolled, and successes are counted against a common threshold. What changes from mission to mission is not how the dice are rolled, but how the successes obtained are spent, as shown further on in section 9.3 and in each mission's specific tables.

Each battery normally rolls 4d12. Every result of 10+ is a success. If the mission is aided by successful aerial observation, it hits on 9+.

Mission Cost Feasible if... Main effect
Preparatory bombardment 3 LP Batteries available, valid target, order written. Suppresses MGs, reduces fortifications, inflicts Fatigue, prepares an assault.
Rolling barrage 2 LP Linked to an assault declared the same turn. Suppresses MGs and may give +1 success to the assault.
Counter-battery fire 2 LP Enemy artillery type chosen. Suppresses or damages enemy batteries.
Harassing fire 1 LP Battery available and valid target sector. Inflicts Fatigue or hinders movement.

9.3 Effects of Artillery Successes

Unless the mission states otherwise, every artillery success can be spent on one of the following effects:

  • suppress 1 MG on the target line;
  • reduce an eligible fortification by 1;
  • inflict +1 Fatigue on a target division;
  • force a morale test;
  • add +1 success to the linked assault, if the mission allows it.

9.4 Artillery Limits

  • Maximum 3 batteries against a single line per turn, unless the scenario states otherwise.
  • Fortification level 4 can only be reduced by heavy artillery or mortars.
  • Hitting Line 2 or Line 3 requires observation, reconnaissance, high ground, or a scenario rule.
  • A battery can carry out only one mission per turn.

9.5 Counter-Battery Fire

A counter-battery mission chooses one type of enemy artillery: field, heavy, or mortars. The assigned batteries roll normally; every 10+ is a success.

Successes Effect
0 No effect.
1-2 One enemy battery of the chosen type is suppressed for this turn.
3-4 Two enemy batteries of the chosen type are suppressed for this turn.
5+ Two batteries are suppressed and one is also damaged in the following turn.

9.6 Rolling Barrage

The rolling barrage must be linked to an assault declared the same turn. It represents fire advancing ahead of the infantry.

Successes Effect
0 No effect; one attacking division gains +1 Fatigue from disorganization.
1-2 One defensive MG on the target line is suppressed.
3-4 One MG is suppressed and the attacker gains +1 success on the assault.
5+ Two MGs are suppressed and the attacker gains +1 success on the assault.

9.7 Harassing Fire

Successes Effect
0 No effect.
1-2 One target division gains +1 Fatigue.
3+ One target division gains +1 Fatigue and the enemy's first move into the sector costs +1 LP.
Example: the German assigns two batteries to C2 L1. He rolls 8d12 at 10+ and gets 3 successes. He uses 1 success to suppress an MG, 1 to inflict +1 Fatigue on F02, and 1 to add a success to the assault.
