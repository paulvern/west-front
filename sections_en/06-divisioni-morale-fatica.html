5.0 Divisions, Morale, and Fatigue

The division is the main unit of the game. It represents not just armed men, but also officers, communications, cohesion, services, the ability to absorb losses, and the will to stay in the line.

For this reason a division is never described by a single number. Men measure its raw firepower; Experience and VT measure the quality of its training and officers; Morale measures its will to keep fighting; Fatigue measures the physical and organizational wear it has accumulated. Two divisions with the same number of men can behave very differently in battle: a Veteran with high Morale and low Fatigue hits more often and holds up better under pressure than a numerically identical Recruit division already worn down by days of combat. The following sections define each of these values and how they interact with one another.

5.1 Division Fields

Field Use
Code Permanent identifier. Does not change even if the division is reduced or reconstituted.
Men Every 1,000 men provide 1d12 in combat. Fractions below 1,000 give no dice.
Experience Determines the division's base VT.
VT Minimum result on d12 needed for a success.
Morale Value from 1 to 5. Affects morale tests, routs, and surrender.
Fatigue Value from 0 to 5. Represents wear, disorder, tiredness, and attrition.
Position Sector and line occupied.

5.2 Men and Dice

Every 1,000 men provide 1d12 in combat. A division with 12,300 men rolls 12d12. A division with 5,900 men rolls 5d12.

Example: G02 has 12,000 men and is Veteran, VT 8+. In combat it rolls 12d12. Every result of 8 or higher is a success.

5.3 Experience and VT

Experience VT Description
Guard 7+ Elite. Rare, costly to reconstitute, and very effective.
Veteran 8+ Experienced. Holds up better under pressure and attacks more effectively.
Regular 9+ Trained. Average quality for operational divisions.
Recruit 10+ Inexperienced. Less effective, more vulnerable to fatigue and low morale.
Militia 11+ Low quality or poor cohesion.

It is worth noting right away how Experience and VT combine with Morale and Fatigue to determine combat effectiveness: VT sets how hard it is to score a single success, while Morale and Fatigue shift it by 1 point one way or the other depending on circumstances. A Veteran division (VT 8+) with low Morale can therefore end up hitting like a Regular division, while a Regular division (VT 9+) with excellent Morale in a prepared attack can temporarily hit like a Veteran. These two values, described in the next two sections, are what makes the same division more or less effective from one turn to the next, regardless of its baseline training.

5.4 Morale

Morale State Effect
5 Excellent May improve VT by 1 in a prepared attack.
4 Good No effect.
3 Unstable No immediate effect.
2 Low VT worsened by 1 when attacking.
1 Broken Cannot attack. Risks routing or surrendering under further pressure.

Morale, then, is not just a bar to be drained: above value 3 it can even give an advantage, while below value 3 it starts to penalize the attack, up to making the division unable to attack at Morale 1. It is the value that most directly distinguishes a division still fit to fight from one at risk of falling apart at the next shock.

5.5 Fatigue

Fatigue Effect
0-1 No effect.
2 Morale recovery becomes harder, if the scenario provides for it.
3 Cannot move and attack in the same turn.
4 VT worsened by 1 when attacking.
5 Exhausted: cannot attack and must be withdrawn as soon as possible.

5.6 Morale Test

While Morale and Fatigue are values that change slowly, usually by 1 point at a time following a specific event, the morale test is the moment when these values are put to a more direct test. It does not measure firepower: it measures whether a division, hit hard, manages to stay cohesive. Good Morale does not guarantee success, but makes it more likely; a division already under strain risks entering a spiral in which every failure adds Fatigue, which in turn makes the next failure more likely.

When a rule calls for a morale test, roll:

1d12 + Morale

Total Outcome
5 or less Serious failure: the division loses 1 Morale and gains +1 Fatigue.
6-8 Failure: the division gains +1 Fatigue.
9-12 Success: no effect.
13+ Full success: no effect; if on defense, also ignores the turn's first +1 Fatigue.

5.7 When to Test Morale

  • When a division suffers at least 2,000 losses in the same combat.
  • When a division is hit by gas and the effect requires it.
  • When a division loses a trench combat.
  • When a division becomes isolated.
  • When a division at Morale 1 would lose further Morale.
  • When a scenario rule requires it.
Example: F02 has Morale 4 and suffers 2,000 losses. It rolls 1d12 and gets 5. Total 9: success. It suffers no further morale effects.
