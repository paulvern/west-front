20.0 Victory Conditions — Messines 1917

Unlike Verdun and the Isonzo, the question here is not so much "who wins" — the British starts with an enormous advantage and would have to play badly to lose it — as "how well does the historical success get replicated, and at what price." A full British victory requires taking the whole ridge within the historical timeframe, stopping exactly where Plumer stopped. A German victory, in a scenario designed to be nearly impossible to win on the ground, is measured mainly in attrition inflicted and time bought.

20.2 German Victory Points

In such a lopsided scenario, German VP do not measure "whether" the ridge falls, but how much it costs the British to take it and how long the Oosttaverne Line can be held — the one objective where German resistance historically had some real effect, forcing Plumer into a week of fighting before he could consolidate.

20.3 Penalties for Disproportionate Losses

Same logic as Verdun (section 16.3) and the Isonzo (section 18.3): the penalty triggers only when losses are both high in absolute terms and lopsided relative to the opponent. The thresholds here are the lowest in the manual, proportioned to a scenario of just 6 turns with the smallest armies presented so far.

Condition Penalty
The British has at least 28,000 losses AND British losses > German losses + 6,000-2 British VP
The British has at least 32,000 losses AND British losses > German losses + 6,000-5 British VP instead of -2
The German has at least 19,000 losses AND German losses > British losses + 6,000-2 German VP
The German has at least 22,000 losses AND German losses > British losses + 6,000-5 German VP instead of -2

In practice, the German almost always crosses his own threshold as early as Turn 1, because of the mines: the German penalty is therefore less a warning about how the game is played and more the mechanical confirmation that at Messines the defender starts out already behind.

20.5 Determining the Winner

At the end of Turn 6, add up both players' VP, apply any penalties, then calculate the difference. The same table used for Verdun and the Isonzo applies unchanged.

VP difference Result
0-3Operational draw.
4-7Marginal victory.
8-11Significant victory.
12+Decisive victory.
Example: at the end of Turn 6, the British operationally controls all eight sectors, reached M8 by Turn 6 (but not all of M1-M7 by Turn 2) and has 24,000 losses against the German's 21,000 — no penalty triggers for him, but the German exceeds his own 19,000 threshold by a gap of only 3,000: no penalty for him either. The British tallies full control of eight sectors plus the pace bonus on M8 plus the "bite and hold" historical success: an almost certainly decisive victory, in line with what really happened in June 1917.
