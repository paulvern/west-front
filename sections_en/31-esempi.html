Part IV — Complete Examples

Practical examples to clarify sequence, actions, costs, and effects.

The eight examples below are ordered to roughly follow the turn sequence described in chapter 3.0: from logistical and air preparation, through artillery and gas, to combat itself and its consequences — retreat, surrender, and finally the reckoning at the end of the scenario. They are not meant to be read in isolation: each one deliberately reuses numbers and situations already encountered in previous chapters, to show how the same rules, presented separately for clarity, behave when applied together in an actual game situation. Anyone still unsure about a specific procedure after reading the corresponding chapter would do well to look up the relevant example here before consulting the abstract rule again.

Example 1 — German Preparation Turn

Turn 1. The German wants to prepare the attack on C2 Beaumont.

  1. Weather and Wind Phase: the roll gives Clear weather, Favorable wind.
  2. He receives the turn's LP and the scenario's +6 special LP.
  3. He pays 3 LP for a preparatory bombardment on C2 L1.
  4. He pays 1 LP for artillery observation on C2 L1.
  5. He pays 1 LP for fighter escort.
  6. He writes his orders: observation, escort, bombardment, movement of G02 and G05 into assault position.
  7. In the Aviation Phase he resolves escort first, then observation.
  8. If the observation succeeds, the bombardment hits on 9+ instead of 10+.
  9. In the Artillery Phase he resolves the bombardment and applies suppression or Fatigue.

Example 2 — Aviation and Artillery

The German assigns a reconnaissance squadron to Artillery Observation on C2 L1 and a fighter squadron to Escort.

  • Observation cost: 1 LP.
  • Escort cost: 1 LP.
  • The Frenchman assigns a fighter to Interception: 1 LP.

The fighter duel is resolved. The German rolls 9, the Frenchman rolls 6. The German wins and applies +1. The Observation mission rolls 6, +1 = 7: it succeeds.

The German bombardment linked to C2 L1 therefore hits on 9+ instead of 10+.

Example 3 — Gas Before the Assault

Turn 2. The German pays 4 LP for Gas on C2 L1. The wind is Favorable.

He rolls 4d12: 4, 9, 11, 12. He gets 3 successes.

The German chooses to:

  • suppress 1 MG;
  • inflict +1 Fatigue on F02;
  • force a morale test on F02.

F02 passes the morale test, but the line is weakened all the same. If the German attacks C2 L1 the same turn, he receives +1 success on the assault because the gas suppressed an MG and inflicted Fatigue.

Example 4 — Attack and Combat

G02, 12,000 men, Veteran, VT 8+, attacks F02, 10,000 men, Regular, VT 9+. An unsuppressed French MG takes part in defensive fire.

  • G02 rolls 12d12 and gets 7 successes.
  • F02 rolls 10d12 and gets 4 successes.
  • The MG rolls 2d12 and gets 1 success.

Attacker total: 7. Defender total: 5. Difference: +2 German. Result: mutual losses, no advance.

At High intensity, every 2 enemy successes cause 1,000 losses. F02 loses 3,000 men. G02 loses 2,000 men.

Example 5 — Contested Retreat

F02 is in C2 L1 and wants to retreat to C2 L2. The Frenchman writes the retreat order. The German writes an attack order with G02 against C2 L1.

After the orders are revealed, Operational Initiative is rolled.

  • If the Frenchman wins, F02 retreats first. G02 may occupy C2 L1 if it is left empty.
  • If the German wins, G02 engages F02 and combat is resolved before the retreat.

Example 6 — Line Without Divisions but With Fortification

D2 L1 contains no French divisions, but has Fortification 3 and 1 MG. G02 attacks.

The MG rolls normally. The fortification provides its local garrison bonus. If the German gets at least 1 net success after defensive fire, he takes D2 L1.

The line cannot retreat, cannot rout, and does not test morale because it contains no divisions.

Example 7 — Surrender Test

F02 is isolated, Morale 1, Fatigue 5, and must retreat, but has no valid route. It must take a surrender test.

Formula: 1d12 + Morale - Fatigue.

F02 rolls 7. Base total: 7 + 1 - 5 = 3. It is isolated: -2. No retreat route: -2. Final total: -1.

Result: full surrender. F02 is removed from the game.

Example 8 — German Historical-Operational Success

At the end of the scenario the German controls C2 Beaumont and operationally controls D2 Douaumont. The French has suffered 22,000 losses and F02/F04 are exhausted.

The German earns:

  • VP for C2;
  • VP for operational control of D2;
  • VP for pace, since C2 and D2 fell within the deadlines;
  • VP for French attrition;
  • VP for exhausted French divisions;
  • the German historical-operational success bonus.

Even without capturing Verdun, the German can achieve a marginal or significant victory if he has kept up the pace, captured symbolic objectives, and worn down the French.

These eight examples do not exhaust every possible combination — no manual could — but they cover the mechanics that raise the most doubts at the table: the interaction between aviation and artillery, the effect of wind on gas, the comparison of successes in combat, and the chain that leads from a defeat to retreat, to rout, or to surrender. With these cases as a reference, and with chapter 3.0 on hand for the exact order of phases, two players should be able to consistently resolve almost any situation that comes up during a game.
