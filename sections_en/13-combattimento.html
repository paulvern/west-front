12.0 Combat

Combat represents fire, advance, losses, suppression, assault, panic, morale endurance, and the ability to keep cohesion under pressure.

This chapter is where almost all the previous systems converge: the artillery preparation of chapter 9.0, any gas from chapter 10.0, the Morale and Fatigue framework of chapter 5.0, the position on the map from chapter 4.0. An assault that looks simple on paper — "the attacker gets more successes than the defender" — is in reality the result of every decision made in the previous phases of the turn. The following procedure should therefore be read not as an isolated event, but as the moment when the investment made in logistics, artillery, and aviation pays off, or is lost.

12.1 Declaring an Attack

Action Cost Feasible if... Procedure Effect
Attack / assault 0 LP base Order written; division not at Fatigue 5; division not at Morale 1; valid target; reachable line. Each division rolls 1d12 for every 1,000 men. Every result equal to or higher than the current VT is a success. Compare attacker's and defender's successes. Apply the results table.

12.1A Simultaneous Fire Limit

Regardless of how many divisions are present or included in the order, at most 2 divisions per side roll dice in the success comparison of 12.4 in a single turn, for the same line — the same limit already set by defensive capacity (chap. 4.3). It represents the fact that, beyond a certain number of men, it is not possible to bring useful, effective fire to bear on the same stretch of front on the same day.

  • If a player has more than 2 divisions eligible to intervene on the same line in the same turn, he indicates in the orders which 2 fight; the others remain in place, do not roll dice, and take no losses that turn.
  • Waiting divisions can be committed in a later turn, without having to repeat the move into the sector.
  • The limit applies only to divisions. MG companies follow their own separate cap (chap. 4.3: maximum 4 per line, unless stated otherwise by the scenario) and continue to fire under 12.3 regardless of this limit.

12.3 Supporting Fire

MG units that are not suppressed and are eligible may fire in support of the combat, both when their own side is attacking and when it is defending. On defense, the fire happens before the main combat, as automatic fire against the incoming assault. On attack, the successes are simply added to the assault's total in the 12.4 comparison.

  • Every MG in the sector rolls 2d12 at 9+, both attacking and defending.
  • Every MG supporting from an adjacent sector rolls 1d12 at 9+, both attacking and defending — it is the same flanking fire, applied depending on which side receives it.
  • A suppressed MG does not roll.
  • An MG on a contested line does not roll.

12.4 Resolving the Attack

This is the table where the value of all the preparation done in the previous phases is concretely measured: every artillery success dedicated to the rolling barrage, every machine gun suppressed in time, every point of Morale saved, weighs directly on the final difference. A modest difference (1-2) is not wasted effort: it still inflicts losses, but is not enough to shift the line. Only from a difference of 3 does the attack start to bite into the ground, with the trench combat described in the next section.

Add up the attacker's and defender's successes. Calculate the difference in the attacker's favor.

Difference in the attacker's favor Result
0 or less Attack repulsed.
1-2 Mutual losses, no advance.
3-4 The attacker enters the line: trench combat.
5-6 Line nearly taken: trench combat with attacker advantage.
7+ Local breakthrough: the defender must retreat or suffer heavy losses.

12.5 Trench Combat

If the result allows the attacker to enter the line, trench combat is resolved.

  • The line's MGs do not fire.
  • The attacker rolls half of the remaining dice, rounded down.
  • The defender rolls all available dice.
  • The defender adds any automatic successes from fortification.
Outcome Result
Defender wins or ties The line holds. The attacker stays outside the line.
Attacker wins by 1-2 The line becomes contested.
Attacker wins by 3 or more The defender suffers a forced retreat.

12.6 Local, Sector, and Operational Breakthrough

Type Condition Effect
Local breakthrough Combat result with a difference of 7+ in the attacker's favor. The defender must retreat or suffer heavy losses.
Sector breakthrough A player controls all 3 lines of a sector that was previously enemy-held. The sector is fully conquered.
Operational breakthrough A player fully or operationally controls an enemy sector and, in a later turn, moves or attacks with at least one division into a deeper connected enemy sector. May award VP or deny scenario defensive bonuses.

12.7 Losses

Losses are calculated based on the scenario's intensity.

Intensity Rule
LowEvery 4 enemy successes cause 1,000 losses.
MediumEvery 3 enemy successes cause 1,000 losses.
HighEvery 2 enemy successes cause 1,000 losses.
Example: G02 gets 7 successes. F02 gets 4 successes and an MG gets 1 success. Defender total 5. German difference +2: mutual losses, no advance. At High intensity, F02 suffers 3,000 losses from the 7 German successes; G02 suffers 2,000 losses from the 5 French successes.
