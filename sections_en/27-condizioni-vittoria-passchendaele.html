26.0 Victory Conditions — Passchendaele 1917

No scenario in this manual should make a victory feel as bitter as this one. Even a full territorial victory, if bought at excessive cost, should not feel like a triumph — which is why the penalty thresholds here are among the lowest in the manual, and fidelity to History matters as much as ground gained.

26.3 Penalties for Disproportionate Losses

Same logic as the other scenarios, but with the tightest thresholds in the manual: here even coming close to the historical cost should carry weight. The British thresholds are set just above the 15,634 real Canadian losses; the German ones reflect the roughly 30,000 German losses estimated for the same period.

Condition Penalty
The British has at least 18,000 losses AND British losses > German losses + 5,000-2 British VP
The British has at least 22,000 losses AND British losses > German losses + 5,000-5 British VP instead of -2
The German has at least 22,000 losses AND German losses > British losses + 5,000-2 German VP
The German has at least 26,000 losses AND German losses > British losses + 5,000-5 German VP instead of -2

26.4 Optional Automatic Victory

Condition Result
The British operationally controls P8 Westrozebeke at any point Immediate decisive British victory — an outcome never reached in reality.

A player has at least 2 divisions surrendered or removed by surrender The opponent achieves an immediate significant victory.

26.5 Determining the Winner

At the end of Turn 6, add up both players' VP, apply any penalties, then calculate the difference. The same table used for the other scenarios applies unchanged.

VP difference Result
0-3Operational draw.
4-7Marginal victory.
8-11Significant victory.
12+Decisive victory.
Example: by the end of Turn 5 the British operationally controls P6 Passchendaele, in time for the pace bonus, having respected the objective limit (section 25.8) for the whole scenario: disciplined historical success earned. Final losses are 17,000 British against 24,000 German — a gap of 7,000, not quite enough for the "the mud hit the Germans harder" bonus (which needs at least 8,000) but still close enough to stay under the British penalty threshold. A British victory that is probably marginal or significant, earned — as in reality — at a price no one would call cheap.
