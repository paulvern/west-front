11.0 Aviation

Aviation is not mainly for destroying things. It is for seeing, correcting artillery fire, protecting friendly missions, disrupting enemy missions, and slowing down movement. Each squadron can carry out only one mission per turn.

Anyone expecting a direct offensive role from aviation, as in later conflicts, will probably be surprised: in 1916 the aeroplane is still, above all, an information multiplier. A successful reconnaissance strips the enemy of surprise; successful observation makes artillery more lethal; local superiority protects other friendly air missions in the same sector. Even the few genuinely "aggressive" actions, such as interdiction, act indirectly, hindering enemy movement or logistics rather than destroying units. Chapters 9.0 and 10.0 show how often aviation works hand in hand with artillery and gas.

Aviation in 1916: during the Great War the aeroplane was above all a tool for reconnaissance and observation. Its greatest impact was making artillery more accurate and spotting enemy movements.

11.2 Air Procedure

Full procedure:
  1. Check the weather in the Weather Phase.
  2. Pay the LP in the Logistics Phase.
  3. Write down mission, squadron, sector, and line if needed.
  4. Reveal orders.
  5. Resolve fighters, escort, interception, and local superiority first.
  6. Resolve reconnaissance, observation, and interdiction.
  7. Apply the effects in the appropriate phase.

11.5 Reconnaissance

With a successful Reconnaissance, the player chooses two pieces of information:

  • which enemy divisions are present in the sector;
  • which lines the enemy divisions are in;
  • how many active MGs are present on a chosen line;
  • the fortification level of a chosen line;
  • whether the sector contains reserves that just entered;
  • whether the sector is isolated or has supply problems.

In addition, a successful Reconnaissance removes, for that turn, the "attacker VT +1 if attacking without reconnaissance" penalty.

11.6 Artillery Observation

Artillery Observation must be linked to an artillery mission declared the same turn. The order must state:

  • the squadron used;
  • the sector observed;
  • the target line;
  • the linked artillery mission.

If it succeeds, the linked artillery mission hits on 9+ instead of 10+.

Limit: an Observation mission can assist only one artillery mission. An artillery mission can receive at most one observation bonus.

11.7 Fighters and Duels

When two opposing air missions meet in the same sector — typically an escort against an interception — the conflict is not resolved with a simple success threshold, but with a direct comparison of the two rolls: whoever gets the higher result gains an advantage that carries over to the mission it was protecting or disrupting.

If both players assign fighters to the same sector, a duel is resolved. Both roll 1d12. The higher result gains local control of the sky.

Difference Result
0 Both fighters interfere with each other: no escort or interception modifier applies.
1-3 The winner applies his own modifier; the loser does not.
4+ The winner applies his own modifier; the loser cannot use that squadron the following turn.

11.8 Interdiction

An Interdiction mission chooses a sector. If it succeeds, the player chooses one of the following effects:

Effect Description
Movement hindered The first enemy movement that enters, leaves, or crosses the sector costs +1 LP.
Fatigue An enemy division that moves in the sector gains +1 Fatigue.
Reserves slowed An enemy reserve entering the sector must stop in Line 3 and gains +1 Fatigue.
Retreat disrupted An enemy division retreating through the sector gains +1 additional Fatigue.
Full example: the German wants to bombard C2 L1. He pays 1 LP for Observation and 1 LP for Escort. The Frenchman pays 1 LP for Interception. The fighters duel: the German wins. The observation receives +1 and succeeds. In the Artillery Phase, the German bombardment on C2 L1 hits on 9+ instead of 10+.
