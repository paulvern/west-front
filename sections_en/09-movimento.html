8.0 Movement

Movement represents a division's ability to change operational position in one day. It does not measure metres covered, but coordination, orders, roads, fatigue, enemy pressure, and logistics.

Precisely because movement is an operational abstraction and not a measure of distance, the following rules focus on what really matters at the level of command represented by the game: what connections exist between sectors, what they cost in LP and time, and what happens when two enemy divisions try to occupy the same space at the same moment. A player does not need to calculate kilometres; he must decide whether it is worth moving a division now, knowing that the move commits it for the rest of the turn and limits its other options.

8.1 Types of Movement

Movement Cost Procedure Effect
Internal movement 0 LP Order written; move of one line within the same controlled sector. The division changes line.
Operational movement 1 LP Order written; move toward an adjacent, connected, friendly sector. The division changes sector.
Entering an enemy sector 0 LP base Requires an attack order. Not normal movement: resolved as combat.
Voluntary retreat 0 or 1 LP Order written; see retreat rules. The division falls back and may gain Fatigue.
Forced retreat 0 LP Consequence of combat, morale, or rout. The division falls back per mandatory priority.

8.2 Movement Restrictions

  • A division at Fatigue 3 or more cannot move and attack in the same turn.
  • A division at Fatigue 5 cannot attack.
  • A division on a contested line cannot move normally: it must fight or retreat per the rules.
  • An isolated division cannot carry out voluntary operational movement, unless stated by the scenario.
  • A division that has just entered from reserve cannot attack the same turn.

8.3 Simultaneous Movement and Conflicts

Situation Result
Two enemy divisions enter the same empty line The line becomes contested; a meeting engagement is resolved.
A division attacks while the defender tries to retreat Operational Initiative decides whether the defender disengages.
Two enemy divisions try to swap sectors The swap is forbidden; both remain in their original sector and gain +1 Fatigue.
Two units enter the same line from different directions Contested line. Defensive bonuses go only to whoever already controlled the line with units present.

8.4 Meeting Engagement

The meeting engagement represents a confused clash, not fully prepared.

Unlike the planned assault of chapter 12.0, which assumes a clearly defined attacker and defender, the meeting engagement arises when neither player yet "owns" the contested line: both arrive there in the same turn, with no time to prepare. For this reason a simpler procedure is used, without fortifications and without the full resolution table of chapter 12.4 — just a direct comparison of net successes, consistent with the tactical confusion the rule is meant to represent.

  • If the line was empty, neither side uses fortifications.
  • MGs fire only if they were already present and controlled by one side at the start of the turn.
  • Both sides roll the dice of the divisions involved normally.
  • With 3 or more net successes, the winner controls the line.
  • With 1-2 net successes, the line remains contested.
  • On a tie, both sides remain committed and gain +1 Fatigue.
Example: G07 enters D2 L1 while F08 enters the same line from F2. The line was empty. Neither side uses fortifications. A meeting engagement is resolved. If the German wins by 3 successes, he controls D2 L1; if he wins by 1-2, the line remains contested.
