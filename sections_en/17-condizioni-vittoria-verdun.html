16.0 Victory Conditions — Verdun 1916

Victory conditions measure ground taken, offensive pace, and attrition inflicted. The German can win even without taking Verdun if he achieves operational success: Beaumont, Douaumont, and serious French attrition.

This chapter closes the scenario by translating everything that happened in the previous turns into a score. It is worth reading before starting to play, not only at the end: knowing from Turn 1 which sectors, which forts, and which offensive pace matter for victory shapes the logistical and operational decisions described in chapter 15.0. A German who ignores the victory conditions risks winning individual combats without getting closer to winning the scenario; a Frenchman who ignores them risks defending secondary sectors while the decisive ones fall.

Historical note: a German victory in the scenario does not necessarily represent the fall of the city. It can represent an operational success: capturing symbolic positions, wearing down the French, and keeping the initiative.

16.3 Penalties for Disproportionate Losses

These penalties exist to discourage a strategy that is otherwise tempting but untrue to the scenario's theme: winning "on paper" by piling up territorial objectives while bleeding one's own army beyond all reason. They apply only when losses are both high in absolute terms and lopsided relative to the opponent: a brutal battle in which both armies bleed at roughly the same rate is not penalized, since that still reflects the mutual attrition the scenario is meant to represent — it is the imbalance, one army being ground down far more than the other, that signals a reckless strategy, not the raw number of losses.

Condition Penalty
The German has at least 35,000 losses AND German losses > French losses + 10,000-2 German VP
The German has at least 40,000 losses AND German losses > French losses + 10,000-5 German VP instead of -2
The French has at least 30,000 losses AND French losses > German losses + 10,000-2 French VP
The French has at least 35,000 losses AND French losses > German losses + 10,000-5 French VP instead of -2

16.4 Optional Automatic Victory

This section is meant for quicker games or for playtesting: instead of always waiting for the final tally at Turn 10, some particularly clear-cut milestones end the game immediately. This is an optional rule: the two players may agree to ignore it and still play all 10 turns through to the end for a fuller contest.

Condition Result
The German controls G2 Verdun at the end of any turn from Turn 6 onward Immediate decisive German victory.
The German fully controls D2 Douaumont, D3 Vaux, and F2 Souville Immediate significant German victory.
The French controls C2 Beaumont, D2 Douaumont, D3 Vaux, and F2 Souville at the end of Turn 10 Decisive French victory.
A player has at least 4 divisions surrendered or removed by surrender The opponent achieves an immediate significant victory.

16.5 Determining the Winner

The final calculation is deliberately simple: there are no hidden multipliers or last-minute exceptions. Add up all the VP earned in the previous sections, subtract any penalties from section 16.3, and compare the difference between the two totals with the table below. A hard-fought game, with objectives that balance out on both sides, will naturally tend toward an operational draw or a marginal victory; only a clear imbalance, on the ground or in attrition, produces a significant or decisive victory.

At the end of Turn 10, add up both players' VP, apply any penalties, then calculate the difference.

VP difference Result
0-3Operational draw.
4-7Marginal victory.
8-11Significant victory.
12+Decisive victory.
