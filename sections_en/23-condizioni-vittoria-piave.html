22.0 Victory Conditions — Piave 1918

The exact reverse of Messines (section 20.0): there the defender had to hope for a miracle, here it is the attacker who must go looking for one. Austro-Hungarian VP measure how far he manages to get before the river — literally — changes the game.

22.3 Penalties for Disproportionate Losses

Same logic as Verdun, the Isonzo, and Messines: the penalty triggers only when losses are both high in absolute terms and lopsided relative to the opponent.

Condition Penalty
The Austro-Hungarian has at least 26,000 losses AND Austro-Hungarian losses > Italian losses + 8,000-2 Austro-Hungarian VP
The Austro-Hungarian has at least 30,000 losses AND Austro-Hungarian losses > Italian losses + 8,000-5 Austro-Hungarian VP instead of -2
The Italian has at least 22,000 losses AND Italian losses > Austro-Hungarian losses + 8,000-2 Italian VP
The Italian has at least 26,000 losses AND Italian losses > Austro-Hungarian losses + 8,000-5 Italian VP instead of -2

Consistent with the historical outcome (Austro-Hungarian losses nearly double Italian losses), the Austro-Hungarian penalty triggers more easily than the Italian one: it is the price of attacking first against a river that, sooner or later, takes the defender's side.

22.5 Determining the Winner

At the end of Turn 7, add up both players' VP, apply any penalties, then calculate the difference. The same table used for the other scenarios applies unchanged.

VP difference Result
0-3Operational draw.
4-7Marginal victory.
8-11Significant victory.
12+Decisive victory.
Example: by the end of Turn 4 the Austro-Hungarian operationally controls P1, P2, P3, P4, and P6 — five of seven sectors, enough for the historical-operational success. From Turn 5 the flood bites: by Turn 7 the Italian has driven the Austro-Hungarian out of every sector except P1, where one isolated division remains at Fatigue 5. The Italian narrowly misses full control (10 VP) but still earns the "at most 2 contested sectors" bonus (5 VP) plus the defensive success for speed of exploitation: a result that, on balance, tips clearly in his favor — just as it really did in June 1918.
