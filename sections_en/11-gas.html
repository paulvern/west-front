10.0 Gas

Gas is a special mission. It can weaken a line, suppress machine guns, increase fatigue, and cause panic, but it does not conquer ground by itself.

This deliberate limitation — gas never takes a line by itself — is the chapter's most important design choice. Historically, gas was a weapon that disorganized and terrified more than it killed decisively, and it remained vulnerable to protection that grew steadily more effective as the war went on. The game system reflects this: gas is an effectiveness multiplier for an infantry assault that must still take place, not a shortcut to avoid one. A player who pays 4 LP for a Gas mission without also having a division ready to attack the same turn wastes most of its potential.

Historical note: by 1916 gas protection was more widespread than in 1915, but it remained imperfect. Gas could still disorganize a defense, especially when combined with bombardment and an immediate assault.

10.2 Full Procedure

Gas Procedure:
  1. Check wind direction and strength in the Weather and Wind Phase.
  2. Pay 4 LP in the Logistics Phase.
  3. Write the order in the Orders Phase, stating the target sector and line.
  4. Resolve the mission in the Artillery and Gas Phase.
  5. Roll 4d12: every 9+ is a success.
  6. Apply any changes due to wind.
  7. Choose the effects obtained.
  8. Apply any gas protection.

10.3 Effect of Wind

Wind, determined at the start of the turn together with weather (chapter 3.2), is what makes gas a risky weapon to plan in advance: a player must decide to pay 4 LP and write the order before knowing for certain whether conditions will be favorable. With an adverse wind, the weapon can literally turn against whoever uses it.

Wind Effect
Favorable The mission is resolved normally.
Variable The mission suffers -1 success. In addition, roll 1d12: on 1-3 it drifts onto an adjacent line of the same sector, if one exists.
Strong The gas disperses. Even if it gets more successes, it can apply a maximum of 2 effects.
Adverse The player may cancel the mission or resolve it against one of his own lines in the starting sector or in the sector stated by the scenario.

10.4 Effects per Success

For every success obtained, the player chooses one effect from the table below. Some effects can be chosen more than once, others have limits.

Effect Limit Description
Suppress MG Repeatable One MG on the target line is suppressed until the end of the turn.
Fatigue Repeatable One division on the target line gains +1 Fatigue.
Morale Once per division One target division takes a morale test. If it fails, it loses 1 Morale.
Disorder Once per line The target line suffers VT +1 on defense until the end of the turn.
Losses Maximum once If the mission got at least 3 total successes, one target division loses 1,000 men.

10.5 Gas Protection

When a division suffers a Morale or Losses effect from gas, roll 1d12 for gas protection.

1d12 Effect
1-4 Protection ineffective: apply the effect normally.
5-9 Partial protection: ignore the Morale loss, or reduce losses to 0.
10-12 Effective protection: ignore the effect; the division gains at most +1 Fatigue if not already assigned.

10.6 Gas and Assault

If a line has been hit by gas the same turn, divisions attacking it receive +1 success on the assault, but only if the gas produced at least one of the following effects:

  • suppressed at least 1 MG;
  • inflicted at least +1 Fatigue on a defending division;
  • caused Disorder on the line;
  • caused a morale test to fail.
Balance rule: gas cannot conquer a line by itself. It can only make an infantry attack more effective.
Example: the German uses gas on C2 Beaumont L1. He rolls 4d12 and gets 4, 9, 11, 12: 3 successes. He chooses to suppress 1 MG, inflict +1 Fatigue on F02, and force a morale test. Since the gas weakened the line, the German assault the same turn receives +1 success.
