17.0 Scenario: Italian Front — Isonzo 1916

Gorizia, Sabotino, and the Karst: an operational scenario for the Battles of the Isonzo.

17.1 Introduction to the Scenario

This scenario represents an Italian offensive on the Isonzo, inspired chiefly by the Sixth Battle of the Isonzo of August 1916. The Italian player must cross or exploit the bridgeheads on the Isonzo, capture dominating heights, and open the way to Gorizia. The Austro-Hungarian player must hold the heights, slow the pace of the offensive, and inflict losses heavy enough to make every Italian gain too costly. Victory conditions are gathered separately, in chapter 18.0.

Historical note: the Battles of the Isonzo were a long series of Italian offensives against strong Austro-Hungarian positions, often anchored on mountains, karst heights, rivers, and trenches cut into rock. The capture of Gorizia in August 1916 was one of the most significant Italian successes of the front.

17.2 Duration

The scenario lasts 8 turns. Each turn represents one day of operations.

17.6 Special Terrain of the Italian Front

17.6.1 The Isonzo and River Crossings

The Isonzo is an operational obstacle. Wherever the scenario states River or Bridgehead, offensive movement is harder and requires preparation.

Action Cost Feasible if... Procedure Effect
Prepared crossing of the Isonzo 2 LP The starting sector is controlled; no flood or prohibitive weather; order written. Pay 2 LP, state starting sector and target sector. A division may attack or enter a line across the river. It suffers +1 Fatigue after the action.
Building or repairing footbridges/bridges 2 LP Starting sector controlled and not bombarded the same turn. Pay 2 LP in the Logistics Phase. The first Italian movement across that connection next turn costs 0 additional LP.
Improvised crossing 0 LP Only if ordered and the player accepts the risk. The division attacks with VT +1 and gains +1 Fatigue before combat. Allows a risky attack without logistical preparation.
Key rule: the river does not always prevent an attack, but it turns the action into a logistics problem. An attack across the Isonzo without preparation can succeed, but it will be costly.

17.6.2 The Karst

The Karst is harsh, rocky terrain, poor in soft cover but rich in trenches cut into stone, sinkholes, observation posts, and positions that are difficult to storm.

Terrain Effect
Karst The defender ignores 1 assault success. Fortifications cost normally, but artillery must obtain 2 successes to reduce a level 3 or 4 fortification by 1.
Karst/Fortified As Karst. In addition, the first Italian attack without a preparatory bombardment suffers VT +1.
Height The defender ignores 1 assault success. Successful reconnaissance or observation cancels this bonus for the turn's first bombardment, but not for combat.
City/Objective The defender receives +1 automatic success in trench combat, unless the line has no divisions.

17.6.3 Austro-Hungarian Caverns and Shelters

In many sectors of the Isonzo, the Austro-Hungarian defense exploited caverns, shelters dug into rock, and positions in depth.

Action or condition Effect
Austro-Hungarian line in Karst or Height with Fortification 3+ Ignores the first Fatigue effect caused by bombardment that turn.
Heavy bombardment or mortars against caverns Can cancel this protection if it obtains at least 3 successes.

17.10 Scenario Special Rules

17.10.1 Italian Preparation

In Turn 1, the Italian may declare an Offensive Plan on one of: I2 Sabotino, I3 Podgora/Oslavia, A4 San Michele. If he does, he pays 3 LP and receives +1 to the initiative roll in Turns 1-2.

17.10.2 Alpini

Action Cost Feasible if... Effect
Alpine assault on a height 1 LP Alpini unit, target Height or Fort/Height, order written. The Alpini unit ignores the first VT penalty from Height or Karst terrain in that turn's combat. It gains +1 Fatigue after combat.

17.10.3 Karst Trenches

In Karst or Karst/Fortified sectors, the defender ignores 1 assault success. This effect does not apply if the line was hit by a preparatory bombardment with at least 4 successes in the same turn.

17.10.4 Gorizia

A3 Gorizia is a City/Objective sector. To get full VP, the Italian must control at least 2 of 3 lines and no line may be contested. If an Austro-Hungarian division remains in a line of the sector, Gorizia is considered contested for the purposes of full VP.

17.10.5 Loss Intensity

The scenario uses High Intensity: every 2 enemy successes cause 1,000 losses.

Historical accuracy note: the following sections focus mainly on the Sixth Battle of the Isonzo (August 1916), but include elements and sectors that were prominent in other phases of the Isonzo campaign (1915-1917). The operational map represents a complete decision-making arc, while compressing events that in reality unfolded across later battles and at different intensities. These historical notes are not rules, except where explicitly stated.

17.11 The Context of the Battles of the Isonzo

The Battles of the Isonzo were a series of twelve Italian offensives against Austro-Hungarian positions along the Isonzo river, in the Friuli-Venezia Giulia region, between June 1915 and November 1917. The Italian strategic objective was the capture of Trieste and opening the way toward Ljubljana and the heart of the Austro-Hungarian Empire.

The geography of the operational theatre was extremely unfavorable to the attacker. The Isonzo river was a significant natural obstacle, the surrounding heights offered dominant defensive positions, and the Karst plateau was brutally harsh terrain where every position had to be taken by frontal assault against trenches cut into rock.

Operational reading: Italian offensive doctrine under General Luigi Cadorna relied on massive artillery preparation followed by concentrated infantry assaults. The Austro-Hungarian defense, led by Svetozar Boroević, exploited the geography, defense in depth, and the ability to withstand enormous losses while holding key positions.

The Sixth Battle of the Isonzo (6-17 August 1916) was the campaign's first major Italian success. The capture of Monte Sabotino on 6 August and the entry into Gorizia on 9 August were celebrated as significant victories, even though the advance stalled shortly beyond the city.

17.12 Summary Chronology of Summer 1916

Historical date Summary historical event Reflected in the scenario as
4-5 August 1916 Intensive Italian artillery preparation on Austro-Hungarian positions around Gorizia and on the Karst. Turn 1: Italian LP bonus for bombardment, observation, and offensive preparation.
6 August Italian assault on Monte Sabotino. The Alpini capture the summit after brutal fighting, opening the way to Gorizia from the northwest. I2 Sabotino is the first critical objective. Its fall unbalances the defense of Gorizia.
7-8 August Italian consolidation on the Sabotino and pressure on Podgora-Oslavia. The Austro-Hungarians begin withdrawing from some forward positions. I3 Podgora/Oslavia represents the second axis of attack toward Gorizia.
9 August Italian troops enter Gorizia. The city is captured after urban fighting, though the eastern bank (the Gorizia bridgehead) remains contested. A3 Gorizia is the scenario's main objective. Its operational control awards significant VP.
9-12 August Intense fighting on the Karst. The Italians attack Monte San Michele, Doberdò, and other Karst positions, suffering extremely heavy losses against very strong defenses. A4 San Michele and A5 Doberdò are high-intensity sectors. The Karst terrain penalizes the attack.
10-17 August The Italians try to exploit their success toward the east and northeast, but the assaults against Monte Santo, San Gabriele, and the Karst positions in depth fail. The offensive runs out of steam. A1 Monte Santo and A2 San Gabriele remain Austro-Hungarian in the base scenario, representing the limits of the Italian advance.

17.13 Local Offensives and the Scenario's Sectors

17.13.1 I1 Plava — Bridgehead on the Isonzo

Historical context: Plava was one of the Italian bridgeheads on the Isonzo, captured in the early phases of the war. It was an essential logistics base for offensives toward Gorizia and the northern heights. Holding and supplying troops across the river was a constant challenge.

In the game, I1 Plava represents the northern starting point of the Italian offensive. Italian divisions must move from here to attack the Sabotino and the surrounding heights.

In game terms: crossings of the Isonzo cost LP. Plava, as an already consolidated bridgehead, reduces this cost for the turn's first movement.

17.13.2 I2 Monte Sabotino — The Decisive Height

Historical context: Monte Sabotino (609 m) dominated Gorizia from the northwest. Its capture on 6 August 1916 by the Alpini of the Sassari Brigade was decisive for the offensive's success. Italian troops attacked at night, surprising the defenders and quickly consolidating the position before counterattacks could arrive.

In the scenario, the Sabotino is the first critical objective. Without its capture, Gorizia remains very hard to threaten, because the Austro-Hungarian defenders keep observation and enfilading fire on the approach routes.

State of the Sabotino Operational meaning
Austro-Hungarian control Gorizia well defended; the Italians suffer penalties attacking from the west.
Italian control Open road to Gorizia; Italian observation over the enemy rear.

17.13.3 I3 Oslavia-Podgora — The Northern Flank

Historical context: the complex of villages and positions of Oslavia and Podgora, north of Gorizia, was the scene of brutal fighting in many of the Battles of the Isonzo. The position allowed a threat to Gorizia from the north, but was well defended and required repeated attacks.

In the scenario, I3 represents a second axis of attack toward Gorizia. If the Italian captures both the Sabotino (I2) and Podgora-Oslavia (I3), the defense of Gorizia becomes untenable for the Austro-Hungarian.

17.13.4 I4 Lucinico — Intermediate Crossing

Historical-operational context: Lucinico was a village south of Gorizia, on the Italian axis linking the Isonzo bridgeheads to the city. Less fortified than the mountain sectors, but still defended and contested.

In the game, I4 is a transit sector. The Italian starts already in control of it, but must use it as a base to attack Gorizia (A3) or the Karst (A4).

17.13.5 I5 Gradisca — Southern Sector

Historical context: Gradisca d'Isonzo was a southern crossing point of the river. The sector was the logistical link for offensives on the Karst and allowed a threat to the southern flank of the Austro-Hungarian defense.

In the scenario, I5 is the starting base for offensives on the Karst (A4, A5). Like Lucinico, it starts under Italian control but requires an investment of LP to sustain divisions attacking eastward.

17.13.6 A1 Monte Santo — The Unconquered Height

Historical context: Monte Santo (Sveta Gora, 682 m) was one of the most contested objectives, never permanently captured by the Italians during the Battles of the Isonzo. Its dominant position, heavy fortifications, and Austro-Hungarian determination made it a symbol of resistance. Only in the defeat of Caporetto (October 1917) did Monte Santo fall, but because of the general Austro-German advance, not because of an Italian frontal assault.

In the scenario, A1 Monte Santo represents a very difficult objective. If the Italian manages to capture it, it means he has achieved an operational success well beyond the historical result of the Sixth Battle.

17.13.7 A2 San Gabriele — The Karst Fortress

Historical context: San Gabriele (646 m) was part of the Austro-Hungarian defensive system east of Gorizia. Like Monte Santo, it withstood every Italian assault until Caporetto. Its positions were integrated with those of Monte Santo, creating an extremely solid defense in depth.

In the game, A2 San Gabriele is the Austro-Hungarian second defensive line. Capturing it means penetrating the enemy rear and threatening the logistics system that sustains the entire Isonzo defense.

17.13.8 A3 Gorizia — The Symbolic Objective

Historical context: Gorizia had enormous military, political, and symbolic value. Its capture in August 1916 was the first great Italian victory on the Isonzo front and was celebrated with enthusiasm. However, the city was devastated, and its capture did not lead to a collapse of the Austro-Hungarian defense, which reorganized shortly beyond it.

In the scenario, A3 Gorizia is the main objective. Operational control of the sector (at least 2 of 3 lines and no contested line) awards significant VP to the Italian. If it remains Austro-Hungarian, the Italian offensive has failed.

State of Gorizia Meaning in the game
Italian operational control At least 2 Italian lines, no line contested. Full VP.
Contested sector At least one contested line, or a significant Austro-Hungarian presence. Reduced VP.
Austro-Hungarian control The Italian offensive has failed. Zero or negative VP for the Italian.

17.13.9 A4 Monte San Michele — The Bloody Karst

Historical context: Monte San Michele (275 m) was probably the bloodiest sector of the entire Isonzo front. Captured and lost repeatedly in different battles, it became synonymous with attrition, desperate frontal attacks, and enormous losses on both sides. The Karst terrain, the trenches cut into rock, and the dominant position made every assault a massacre.

In the scenario, A4 San Michele is an extremely high-casualty sector. The Karst/Fortified terrain penalizes the Italian attack, but its capture is necessary to advance in depth toward the Vallone del Carso (A6).

Key rule: in Karst sectors the defender ignores 1 assault success. This makes A4 San Michele extremely costly to capture without massive artillery preparation.

17.13.10 A5 Doberdò — The Caverns of the Karst

Historical context: Doberdò (Doberdob) was a Karst sector where Austro-Hungarian troops exploited natural caverns and shelters dug into rock to withstand Italian bombardments. Even after intense artillery preparation, the defenders would emerge from their shelters and repel the assaults.

In the scenario, A5 Doberdò represents Karst defense at its peak. The special rules on Austro-Hungarian caverns mean that divisions in this sector ignore the first Fatigue effect from bombardment if the fortification is 3+.

17.13.11 A6 Vallone del Carso — Rear Area and Depth

Historical-operational context: the Vallone represents Austro-Hungarian strategic depth on the Karst. Reserves, logistics rear areas, and second defensive lines were organized in this belt. Capturing it would mean penetrating beyond the main defensive belt and threatening Trieste itself.

In the game, A6 Vallone del Carso is the final sector. If the Italian manages to capture it, it means he has achieved an operational success well beyond the historical result of the Sixth Battle. It is the entry point for Austro-Hungarian reserves and the heart of the defensive system.

Scenario reading: if the Italian controls Gorizia (A3) and at least two of San Michele (A4), Doberdò (A5), and the Vallone (A6), he has achieved a decisive victory that never actually happened in real history.
