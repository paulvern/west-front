2.0 Full Catalogue of Actions

This section is the main reference. Every possible action is listed with its cost, phase, conditions, procedure, and effect. The detailed rules in the following chapters explain special cases.

It is recommended to treat this chapter as a quick reference during play, not as preparatory reading: each row answers the five questions of chapter 1.0 in compressed form, and implicitly refers back to the chapter of the base rulebook where the procedure is explained in full, with examples. The actions are grouped by functional area — logistics, movement, combat, aviation, gas/morale/supply — because in practice a player tends to decide by area: first how much logistics to devote to the turn's operations, then how to move divisions, then where and how to attack, and so on. Keeping chapter 3.0 (Turn Sequence) at hand as well helps to understand the order in which these actions, even though listed here by category, will actually be resolved at the table.

2.1 Logistical and Administrative Actions

Actions in this category do not move troops or cause combat: they regulate the flow of Logistics Points that makes everything else possible. Receiving, holding, and spending LP is an activity repeated every turn, and one that often decides, well in advance, how long an offensive can be sustained. The arrival of reserves, the assignment of replacements, and the construction of fortifications also belong here, because they share the same administrative nature: they are decisions that prepare the ground for later turns rather than resolve something immediately.

Action Phase Cost Feasible if... How it's done Effect
Receive LP Logistics 0 LP Every turn, per the scenario. Add the received LP to the stored LP, respecting the maximum stockpile. LP available to pay for actions.
Store LP End of Logistics 0 LP The total does not exceed the maximum stockpile. Note down unspent LP. The LP remain available next turn.
Reserve entry Logistics + Orders + Movement 2 LP The division is available and the entry sector is valid. Pay 2 LP, write the order, place in Line 3 of the entry sector. The division enters the map; it cannot attack the same turn.
Assign replacements Logistics 1 LP per 1,000 men The division is not in Line 1, is not contested, is not isolated, and has access to the rear. Pay LP and add men to the division. Increases available manpower. If it receives recruits, it may lose experience.
Build/repair fortification Logistics 2 LP per level Controlled line, not contested, not bombarded the same turn. Pay 2 LP and increase the level by 1, up to the allowed maximum. Improves the line's defense.
Extra Fatigue recovery Recovery 1 LP The division is eligible for base recovery. Pay 1 LP during the Recovery Phase. Removes 1 additional Fatigue.
Declare Offensive Plan Orders 3 LP per turn of preparation The player indicates a main sector and secondary sectors. Write the plan and pay for at least 2 turns of preparation. Once activated, gives bonuses to prepared attacks.

2.2 Movement Actions

Moving a division looks like the simplest action in the game, but the system carefully distinguishes between moving within one's own sector, moving toward a friendly sector, and attempting to enter an enemy sector. Only the first two are "movement" in the strict sense; the third, as the table shows, is not movement at all but an attack disguised as a move, and is resolved with the combat rules of chapter 12.0. This distinction avoids ambiguity on the field: a division never "slides" into a contested line without fighting.

Action Phase Cost Feasible if... How it's done Effect
Internal movement Movement 0 LP Destination line in the same friendly sector, not full and not forbidden. Write the order and move the division one line. The division changes line within the same sector.
Operational movement Movement 1 LP Adjacent, connected, friendly sector; division not blocked by Fatigue or combat; destination line with capacity available (chap. 4.3). Also used to replace a line left uncovered after a local division has advanced. Pay 1 LP, write the order, move to Line 3 or an allowed line. The division changes sector. If the destination line no longer has capacity at the time of resolution, the move fails, the division stays in its starting sector, and the LP spent is lost regardless.
Enter enemy sector Combat 0 LP + any support An attack order is written and there is a valid enemy target line. Does not move like normal movement: an assault is resolved. If the attack succeeds, the attacker may enter the line.
Move 1 MG company Logistics + Movement 1 LP Starting and arrival lines controlled, not contested; valid route. Pay 1 LP and indicate origin/destination. The MG changes line or sector per the scenario; it does not fire if it has moved.
Voluntary retreat Orders + Movement 0 LP within the same internal line; 1 LP toward an adjacent sector Unit not isolated, not contested, with a valid route. Write the retreat order and move according to priority. The division falls back; it may gain Fatigue.
Forced retreat Retreats 0 LP A combat or morale result imposes it. Follow the Retreat Routes table. The division falls back, loses Morale/gains Fatigue; if impossible, tests for surrender.

2.3 Combat and Ground Support Actions

Combat proper is almost always the endpoint of a longer preparation: a bombardment that suppresses machine guns, a rolling barrage that accompanies the assault, counter-battery fire that reduces the enemy's artillery response. The table below lists these actions together, from automatic defensive fire to the advance that follows a victory, to show how they fit into the same causal sequence: first the ground is prepared with artillery, then the assault is declared, then the immediate consequences on the field are managed.

Action Phase Cost Feasible if... How it's done Effect
Attack / assault Orders + Combat 0 LP base The division may attack an adjacent target or the indicated line. Write the order, indicate divisions and target line, resolve the combat. May cause losses, a contested line, enemy retreat, or an advance.
MG supporting fire Combat 0 LP MG not suppressed, line not contested, valid target; applies whether the line is attacking or being attacked (12.3). Every MG rolls 2d12 in its own sector or 1d12 in support from an adjacent sector. Successes are added to the total of the side the MG belongs to, whether attacking or defending.
Preparatory bombardment Artillery 3 LP Batteries available, valid target, order written. Each battery rolls 4d12; success on 10+, or 9+ with observation. Suppresses MGs, reduces fortifications, inflicts Fatigue, triggers a morale test, or gives an assault bonus.
Rolling barrage Artillery + Combat 2 LP Linked to an assault declared the same turn. Roll the assigned batteries and apply the barrage table. Suppresses MGs and may give +1 success to the assault.
Counter-battery fire Artillery 2 LP Batteries available and enemy artillery type chosen. Roll the assigned batteries; every 10+ is a success. Suppresses or damages enemy batteries.
Harassing fire Artillery 1 LP Battery available, valid target sector. Roll the battery's dice. Inflicts Fatigue or increases the enemy's movement cost.
Advance after combat Retreats and advances 0 LP The combat result allows it and the line is not full. Move the winning unit into the conquered line. The attacker occupies or contests the line.

2.4 Air Actions

Aviation, as chapter 11.0 explains more fully, rarely decides the outcome of a turn by itself: its job is to make the other arms more effective. A successful reconnaissance strips the enemy of the advantage of surprise, successful observation makes artillery more accurate, and a successful escort or interception decides who controls the sky over a sector for that turn. These are relatively cheap actions in LP terms, but their value is measured in the effects they unlock elsewhere, not in the direct damage they deal.

Action Phase Cost Feasible if... How it's done Effect
Reconnaissance Aviation 1 LP Reconnaissance squadron available, weather allows flight. Write down the sector; roll 1d12, success on 7+. You gain two pieces of information about the sector and cancel any "no reconnaissance" penalty.
Artillery observation Aviation 1 LP Reconnaissance squadron available and a linked artillery mission. Indicate sector, line, and linked bombardment; roll 1d12, success on 7+. The linked artillery mission hits on 9+ instead of 10+.
Fighter escort Aviation 1 LP Fighter squadron available and a friendly mission in the same sector. Resolve any fighter duel; if successful, apply +1. The friendly air mission receives +1.
Interception Aviation 1 LP Fighter squadron available and an enemy mission in the same sector. Resolve the duel or the fighter mission's fire. The enemy air mission receives -1.
Local superiority Aviation 1 LP Fighters available, sector indicated. Roll 1d12, success on 7+. Friendly air missions in the sector receive +1 during the phase.
Air interdiction Aviation 2 LP Squadron eligible and weather allows flight. Indicate the sector; roll 1d12, success on 7+. Increases the enemy's movement cost or inflicts Fatigue.

2.5 Gas, Morale, Supply, and Surrender

This last group brings together very different actions — a chemical weapon, a psychological test, an end-of-turn administrative check — but they all share the same role: they measure how much longer a division can hold out. Gas weakens the enemy before the infantry attacks; the morale test checks whether a unit holds after taking a blow; the supply check establishes whether a division is still connected to its logistics organization; the surrender test is the last resort, when retreat is no longer possible. The victory check, finally, closes the turn by translating everything that happened into a score.

Action / Check Phase Cost Feasible if... How it's done Effect
Gas mission Artillery and Gas 4 LP Scenario allows gas, wind does not prevent it, order written. Roll 4d12, success on 9+; apply effects per success. Suppresses MGs, inflicts Fatigue, triggers a Morale test, disorder, or limited losses.
Morale test When required 0 LP Losses, gas, isolation, combat, or a rule requires it. Roll 1d12 + Morale. May cause Fatigue, loss of Morale, retreat, rout, or surrender.
Supply check Isolation and supply 0 LP Every division on the map. Trace a path through friendly sectors to a valid rear area. If isolated: no replacements/support, -1 Morale and +1 Fatigue.
Base recovery Recovery 0 LP Division not in L1, not contested, not isolated, not bombarded, has not attacked. Remove 1 Fatigue. The division recovers efficiency.
Surrender test Retreats / Supply 0 LP No retreat route, critical isolation, or surrender conditions. Roll 1d12 + Morale - Fatigue and modifiers. Holds out, routs, partial surrender, or removal.
Victory check End of turn 0 LP Every turn or end of scenario. Calculate VP per the scenario. Determines the level of victory.
Consistency note: an action may cost 0 LP but still require an order. For example, the base attack costs 0 LP, but it must be written in the orders. Without an order, the division does not attack.
