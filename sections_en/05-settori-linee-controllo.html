4.0 Sectors, Lines, and Control

Each sector is an abstract operational area. It does not represent a fixed distance, but a set of positions, trenches, villages, heights, woods, roads, and defensive works. Each sector contains three internal lines: Line 1, Line 2, and Line 3.

This abstraction is deliberate. Representing every single trench, communication trench, or machine-gun nest would have made the game unmanageable without adding any interesting decisions: what matters operationally is not the exact metre of ground, but the depth of the defense. A sector with three lines full, fortified, and connected to the rear is much harder to conquer than the same sector with only one line manned, even though on the map they occupy the same space. Thinking in terms of sectors and lines, rather than precise coordinates, is also more faithful to how 1916 commands actually reasoned: a general rarely knew the exact disposition of every company, but he always knew which positions were held "in depth" and which were not.

4.1 The Three Lines

Line Operational meaning Typical use
Line 1 First position in contact with the enemy. Forward defense, MGs, bombardments, main assaults.
Line 2 Second position, or support line. Local reserves, counterattacks, absorbing retreats.
Line 3 Sector's rear line. Reserve entry, last-ditch defense, connection to the rear.

The three lines are not interchangeable: they represent a progression, from the trench in contact with the enemy to the area reinforcements come from. An attacker who breaks Line 1 has not yet won the sector; he must then face Line 2, where the defender typically keeps his own local reserves for the counterattack, and only afterward Line 3, the last obstacle before the sector is entirely lost. This structure is why, as will be seen in chapter 12.0, even a winning attack rarely conquers an entire sector in a single turn.

4.2 Control of Lines

Situation Control
Line with only one player's units Line controlled by that player.
Line with both players' units Contested line.
Empty line Keeps its previous control, unless the scenario states otherwise.
At least one contested line in the sector The sector is contested for purposes of victory, supply, and operational movement.
A player controls at least 2 of 3 lines and none is contested That player operationally controls the sector.
A player controls all 3 lines Full control of the sector.

4.3 Capacity

Capacity limits the concentration of troops and support elements in a single line. This avoids unrealistic pile-ups and makes managing depth important.

Element Limit
Friendly divisions on an uncontested line Maximum 2.
Divisions per side on a contested line Maximum 2 per player.
MG companies on a line Maximum 4, unless stated otherwise by the scenario.
Fortification Only one level per line.

This same limit also governs Operational Movement (chap. 3.0) when it is used to replace a line left uncovered after a local division has advanced: if the destination line has no free capacity, the movement fails and the LP spent is lost. It also governs how many divisions can actually roll dice in a single turn of combat (chap. 12.1A).

4.4 Lines Without Divisions but With MGs or Fortification

Some lines may contain no divisions, but may still have machine guns, fortifications, garrison detachments, observers, engineers, or local elements. These lines are not considered completely empty for purposes of combat.

This distinction avoids an unnatural behavior often seen in simpler wargames: the idea that a line "without troops" is automatically undefended and can be crossed without consequence. In the reality of the Western Front, a lightly manned line of trenches could still hold fixed machine-gun emplacements, small engineer detachments, or artillery observers able to offer resistance for a limited time. The following rules distinguish four cases, depending on whether the line has only machine guns, only fortification, both, or neither.

Line situation Effect
Line with no divisions, no MGs, and no fortification If attacked by an enemy division, it is automatically occupied.
Line with no divisions but with MGs The MGs may fire defensively. If the attacker gets at least 1 net success after defensive fire, the line is taken.
Line with no divisions but with fortification The line uses the fortification bonus as a local garrison. If the attacker gets at least 1 net success, the line is taken.
Line with no divisions but with MGs and fortification The line uses both the MGs and the fortification bonus. If the attacker gets at least 1 net success, the line is taken.
Key rule: a line without divisions cannot retreat, cannot rout, and cannot test morale. If it is overrun, MGs and fortifications are captured, eliminated, or abandoned according to the outcome of the combat.

4.5 Fortified Sectors and Forts

In sectors with Fort or Fort/Height terrain, operational control may not coincide with full control for Victory Point purposes. A fort can be operationally dominated but not yet fully secured.

Situation Operational control Fort VP
A player controls 2 of 3 lines and no line is contested Operationally controls the sector. Gains the operational value stated by the scenario.
No enemy division remains in the sector Full control. Gains the full value stated by the scenario.
At least one line is contested Contested sector. No full fort VP.
Example: the German controls D2 Douaumont L1 and L2, but French F04 remains in D2 L3. The German operationally controls Douaumont, but does not fully control it.
