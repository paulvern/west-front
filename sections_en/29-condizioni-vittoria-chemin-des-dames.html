28.0 Victory Conditions — Chemin des Dames 1917

In no other scenario in this manual should a full territorial victory leave so little room for satisfaction. French VP reward the army's discipline and cohesion more than the ground it captures — because that is exactly the standard by which History has judged this offensive.

28.3 Penalties for Disproportionate Losses

Same logic as the other scenarios, but with the lowest thresholds in the entire manual. The historical judgment on this offensive was almost immediate: already by the end of April, with French losses past 130,000 for an advance of a few hundred metres, even many contemporaries judged it unjustifiable. The thresholds here reflect that judgment.

Condition Penalty
The French has at least 16,000 losses AND French losses > German losses + 5,000-2 French VP
The French has at least 20,000 losses AND French losses > German losses + 5,000-5 French VP instead of -2
The German has at least 20,000 losses AND German losses > French losses + 5,000-2 German VP
The German has at least 24,000 losses AND German losses > French losses + 5,000-5 German VP instead of -2

28.4 Optional Automatic Victory

Condition Result
The French operationally controls G8 Laon at any point Immediate decisive French victory — an outcome never reached in reality, not even for the rest of 1917.

28.5 Determining the Winner

At the end of Turn 8, add up both players' VP, apply any penalties, then calculate the difference. The same table used for the other scenarios applies unchanged — with the caveat that, here more than anywhere else, the numerical result tells only part of the story.

VP difference Result
0-3Operational draw.
4-7Marginal victory.
8-11Significant victory.
12+Decisive victory.
Example: by the end of Turn 2 the French operationally controls only G1: the morale test in section 27.7 fires on every French division. From then on, average Morale drifts slowly downward; by Turn 5 three divisions are at Morale 2, and the "We will not attack anymore" rule triggers for the first time. The French player spends the following turns letting Morale recover instead of attacking, and manages to drop below the threshold by Turn 7 — enough to avoid triggering it for 3 consecutive turns, but not enough to earn the "the army held together" bonus. By the end of the scenario he controls 4 of the 7 sectors (historical success, measured, earned) but never comes close to Laon. A result that, by the numbers, might read as a marginal French victory — but that, told in words, remains what it really was: an army that stopped, if only for a moment, believing in its own commanders.
