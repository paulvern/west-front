14.0 Fortifications, Supply, and Recovery

Fortifications represent trenches, shelters, strongpoints, forts, and field works. They do not make a position invulnerable, but they increase its ability to absorb fire and hold out.

This chapter is the natural close of the base rulebook, bringing together three topics that, while different, all concern the long-term "health" of the forces in play: how hard it is to dislodge a division from a well-fortified position, how well it stays connected to its logistics organization, and how quickly it can recover from accumulated fatigue. These are three factors a player tends to underrate in early games, focused as he is on artillery and assaults, but over the long run of a scenario like Verdun 1916 they weigh as much as, if not more than, any single combat won or lost.

14.1 Fortification Levels

Level Description Effect
0 No significant works No effect.
1 Light trenches +1 automatic success in trench combat.
2 Deep trenches +1 automatic success and ignores 1 loss.
3 Prepared system +2 automatic successes and ignores 1 loss.
4 Fort +2 automatic successes, ignores 1 loss, requires 2 successes from eligible artillery to lose 1 level.

14.2 Building or Repairing Fortifications

Action Cost Feasible if... Effect
Build/repair 1 level 2 LP Controlled line, not contested, not bombarded the same turn, logistics connection present. Increases the level by 1, up to the allowed maximum.

14.3 Control of Forts for VP Purposes

Sectors with Fort or Fort/Height terrain are harder to consider fully conquered. A fort can be operationally dominated but not yet fully secured.

Situation Operational control Fort VP
A player controls 2 of 3 lines and no line is contested Operationally controls the sector. Gains the operational value stated by the scenario.
No enemy division remains in the sector Full control. Gains the full value stated by the scenario.
At least one line is contested Contested sector. No full fort VP.

14.4 Isolation and Supply

Controlling a line or a sector, by itself, is not enough to guarantee a division's survival: what also matters is whether that position stays connected to the rest of the deployment. A division that has "won" its own sector but finds itself surrounded by enemy territory is not in a better position than one that has lost it; indeed, the isolation described here can bring it to collapse faster than a direct defeat on the field, as shown by its link to the surrender test of chapter 13.6.

A division is supplied if it can trace a path of friendly sectors through to a valid rear area. The path cannot cross sectors controlled by the enemy. A contested sector can be used only if the scenario allows it.

An isolated division:

  • receives no replacements;
  • receives no artillery support;
  • cannot carry out voluntary operational movement, unless stated by the scenario;
  • loses 1 Morale and gains +1 Fatigue at the end of the turn;
  • if at Morale 1 or Fatigue 5, must test for surrender.

14.5 Recovery

Recovery is the only moment of the turn in which a division's state improves rather than worsens, which is why the eligibility conditions are fairly restrictive: it represents the time and resources needed to reorganize units, rotate out the harshest guard shifts, and let the men rest away from direct contact with the enemy. A division constantly committed to the front line never recovers, one more reason why rotating one's forces between Line 1 and the rear is often more effective, in the long run, than keeping the same divisions always at the front.

A division can recover if:

  • it is not in Line 1;
  • it is not on a contested line;
  • it has not attacked this turn;
  • it has not been bombarded this turn;
  • it is not isolated;
  • it is not routed.
Action Cost Effect
Base recovery 0 LP Removes 1 Fatigue.
Extra recovery 1 LP Removes 1 additional Fatigue.
Example: F04 is in D2 L3, not contested, not isolated, has not attacked, and was not bombarded. During the Recovery Phase it removes 1 Fatigue. By spending 1 LP, the Frenchman can remove one additional point of Fatigue.
