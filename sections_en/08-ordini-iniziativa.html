7.0 Simultaneous Orders and Operational Initiative

Orders are written secretly and revealed simultaneously. This represents the uncertainty of command: no player knows for certain whether the enemy will attack, fall back, use reserves, or mass artillery.

Simultaneous writing is the heart of the game's decision-making system: there is no "active player" and a passive one, as in many classic board games. Both plan at the same moment, without knowing the other's intentions, and both must live with the consequences of a plan written before knowing how the opponent will react. This is what makes the aerial reconnaissance phase of chapter 11.0 useful, and not merely decorative: reducing uncertainty before writing orders has real, concrete value.

7.1 Writing Orders

Every order must be clear. It must state the unit, the starting sector, the target sector or line, any support, and the LP paid.

Order type Must state
Movement Division, starting sector/line, destination sector/line.
Attack Attacking divisions, target sector and line, any support.
Voluntary retreat Division, starting line, and preferred destination.
Artillery Mission, assigned batteries, target sector and line.
Gas Sector, target line, and LP paid.
Aviation Squadron, mission, sector and line if needed.
Reserve Division, entry sector, and LP paid.
Example of a valid order: "G02 and G05 attack C2 Beaumont L1 from B1. Preparatory bombardment on C2 L1 with 1 heavy battery and 1 field battery. Squadron R1 observes C2 L1. Fighter C1 escorts R1."

7.2 Determining Operational Initiative

Once orders are revealed, both players already know what the other intends to do: the uncertainty about intentions is over. What remains is to establish in what order to resolve events when two actions touch each other — an attack against a division that is retreating, two divisions entering the same empty line, movements that rule each other out. Operational Initiative answers this remaining question.

After orders are revealed, both players roll 1d12 and apply modifiers. The higher result gains Operational Initiative for the turn.

Condition Modifier
Active Offensive Plan+2
Local air superiority in the main sector+1
Higher offensive LP spending this turn+1
Defender in a fortified sector+1, only in the sector concerned
Divisions involved at Fatigue 4 or more-1
Attacker in fog or mud-1

In case of a tie, the player who held the initiative in the previous turn keeps it. If it is the first turn, initiative is assigned by the scenario.

7.3 What Initiative Decides

  • Who resolves conflicting movements first.
  • Whether a voluntary retreat succeeds before the attacker engages the defender.
  • Who enters a line first when two simultaneous movements collide.
  • The order in which interdependent combats are resolved.
  • Priority in cases not explicitly covered by the rules.
Limit: Operational Initiative never allows orders already written to be changed. It only serves to resolve the order of events.

7.4 Contested Retreat

If a division has orders to retreat and an enemy division has orders to attack it the same turn, Operational Initiative decides whether the defender disengages first or is caught.

Who has initiative Result
Defender The voluntary retreat resolves first. The attacker may occupy the line if it is left empty.
Attacker The attacker catches the defender. Combat is resolved before the retreat.
Example: G02 attacks C2 L1. F02 has orders to retreat from C2 L1 to C2 L2. If the Frenchman has initiative, F02 retreats first. If the German has initiative, G02 catches F02 and the combat takes place.
