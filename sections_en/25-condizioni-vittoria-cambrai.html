24.0 Victory Conditions — Cambrai 1917

Unlike the other three scenarios, here neither player starts with an advantage that lasts the whole game: the British has an enormous one in the early turns, the German an equally real one from Turn 5. VP measure which of the two manages to make his moment of advantage permanent — something that, historically, neither side fully managed to do.

24.3 Penalties for Disproportionate Losses

Same logic as the other three scenarios: the penalty triggers only when losses are both high in absolute terms and lopsided relative to the opponent. Consistent with the near-even balance of the real battle (roughly 40,000 losses per side), the thresholds here sit close together instead of being sharply different as at Verdun or the Piave.

Condition Penalty
The British has at least 22,000 losses AND British losses > German losses + 6,000-2 British VP
The British has at least 26,000 losses AND British losses > German losses + 6,000-5 British VP instead of -2
The German has at least 20,000 losses AND German losses > British losses + 6,000-2 German VP
The German has at least 24,000 losses AND German losses > British losses + 6,000-5 German VP instead of -2

24.4 Optional Automatic Victory

Condition Result
The British operationally controls C8 Cambrai at any point Immediate decisive British victory — an outcome never even approached in reality.
The German controls B1 or B2 at any point Immediate decisive German victory — the historical counterattack never got that far.
A player has at least 3 divisions surrendered or removed by surrender The opponent achieves an immediate significant victory.

24.5 Determining the Winner

At the end of Turn 8, add up both players' VP, apply any penalties, then calculate the difference. The same table used for the other scenarios applies unchanged.

VP difference Result
0-3Operational draw.
4-7Marginal victory.
8-11Significant victory.
12+Decisive victory.
Example: by the end of Turn 4 the British operationally controls all eight sectors except C8, including Bourlon Wood in time for the pace bonus. From Turn 5 the counterattack bites: by Turn 8 the German has recaptured C1, C2, C4, and C6 (four of seven, not five: no German historical success bonus), while the British still holds C3, C5, and C7 — exactly half of the seven, enough for the "beyond History" bonus. A balanced result, with both players close to their own penalty threshold: exactly the kind of knife-edge game the real battle actually was.
